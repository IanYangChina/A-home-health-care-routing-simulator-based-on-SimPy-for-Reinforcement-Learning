Round 8 · Guided exploration with longer returns

PI direction and measured experiment report

20 Nov 2026 · family runner logs 16:06:17–16:17:15 UTC

PI input

15:58:52 UTC · 16:05:00 UTC

ny asked whether Round 7 had used the strongest guidance strategies. Round 7 had kept exploration fixed to compare the waiting costs and return horizons.

ny requested training both agents on all four instances with longer return estimates, a higher waiting penalty, short-wait guidance, and ACO guidance.

Agent work

Kept waiting and return settings fixed within each layout and agent family and compared the exploration control with short-wait and ACO guidance. All arms charged 0.15 points per appointment waiting minute. Masked DQN used 32-decision targets on C and 16-decision targets on A, B, and D, based on Round 7 results for C and D. All PPO arms used 512-decision rollouts with λ=1 and the tuned discount. PPO bootstrapped at rollout boundaries within a shift. The simulator treated the modeled shift end as terminal in training. DQN mixed guidance into epsilon exploration. PPO used imitation warm starts in its guided arms, then collected on-policy rollouts.

Computation

Ran 72 fits across A–D, each for 12,288 steps, using three independent training seeds. Recorded 6,309 complete training episodes, evaluated 864 learned-policy shifts, and reused 96 matched Random and ACO shifts. The route explorers use predeclared training seed 11.

Findings

On A, uniform DQN led at the 0.15 score. Both DQN guides reduced waiting and served fewer visits. Short-wait PPO led the learned A arms under the original 0.05 score. On B and C, short-wait DQN was the strongest learned arm on both scales. On D, short-wait PPO was the strongest learned arm, beating Random across all three training seeds. Standalone ACO led on every layout.

On A, replay of DQN training seed 13 across the 12 test shifts found voluntary deferral on 31.6% of eligible uniform decisions and roughly 82% of guided decisions. On the test seed 101 schedules, one scratch PPO checkpoint served just one visit on C and eight on D, deferring on more than 99% of eligible decisions. The reduced waiting on those schedules reflects visits that never started.

PI-directed diagnostics

21 Nov 2026

Training and baseline diagnostic

ny asked for loss and reward trends and for the dimensions where DQN and PPO underperformed Random or ACO.

ACO led on all four layouts, with the largest coverage and waiting gaps on C and D. DQN loss fell even where reward declined, particularly on D. PPO value loss fell while training rewards mostly plateaued. On the 12 matched test shifts, sampling instead of choosing PPO’s most probable action recovered the collapsed scratch checkpoint from 1 to 83 served visits on C and from 8 to 185 on D.

Observation and return diagnostic

ny asked whether agents could see elapsed and remaining shift time, the full unserved pool, the nurse roster and individual release times, and whether the return targets reached far enough ahead.

The agents saw the clock and every job’s status, plus details of the current nurse and the aggregate busy fraction. Other nurses’ assignments, queue positions and release forecasts were absent. Across the three DQN variants on D, seed 11’s 16-decision targets covered a median of 17–27 simulated minutes and only 55–59% of eventually completed visits finished within their assignment’s direct target. PPO included shift end in about 70% of decisions in completed training shifts on D and 89–90% on A–C.

These findings informed the Round 9 design.
