Round timeline

Each round groups PI input, agent implementation, computation, findings, and PI feedback. Timing uses the available UTC runner logs and result-file times.

  1. Round 1

    First comparison on instance A

    PI input 08:55:52 UTC

    Compute timing unavailable for the initial comparison.

    PI input · ny
    ny asked to train reinforcement learning agents and compare them with fixed dispatch policies.
    Agent work
    Compared the fixed dispatch policies with the learned agents on instance A. Each RL action selected a rank in the current list of eligible appointments.
    Computation
    Instance A contained 60 appointments. GPU training used four Optuna trials and 16,384 final steps per algorithm. The comparison averaged 12 held-out seeds.

    DQN led RL validation. On the held-out seeds it averaged 54.50 visits and 438.08 reward. Random averaged 56.92 visits and 484.41 reward. Earliest deadline and ant colony completed all 60 visits on every seed.

    PI feedback · 09:09:04 UTC
    “Try increasing the network size and also experiment on the other 3 instances.”

  2. Round 2

    Wider networks and three more layouts

    PI input 09:09:04 UTC · 13:28:12 UTC

    Compute timing unavailable for these rank-action runs.

    PI input · ny
    ny asked to increase network size and run the other three instances.
    Agent work
    Kept the initial hyperparameters and budget for the paired A comparison, doubled both hidden layers to 128 units, and made separate tuned runs on B, C, and D. Vectorised simulator checks for larger instances.
    Computation
    A used 16,384 final steps per width and one matched training seed. B, C, and D each used three Optuna trials and 8,192 final steps per algorithm. Held-out means used 12 seeds per configuration.

    On A, DQN stayed at 54.50 mean visits as reward rose by 0.73. PPO rose from 54.42 to 54.83 visits and gained 6.46 mean reward, with six test seeds improving and six declining. Ant colony led the fixed policies on B, C, and D.

    PI feedback · 13:28:12 UTC · 13:32:07 UTC
    ny asked what the agents could observe and do. After seeing that an action represented a changing rank, ny raised the concern that this could impede learning and asked about observation normalization and loss convergence.

  3. Round 3

    Stable appointment IDs and loss logging

    PI input 13:32:07 UTC · 13:47:20 UTC

    20 Nov 2026 · first logged Optuna study 13:35:50, last results file 13:48:32 UTC

    PI input · ny
    ny questioned whether a changing eligible-job rank would let the agent learn reliably and asked whether observations were normalized and losses converged.
    Agent work
    Mapped action j to appointment j throughout a shift. DQN and PPO applied the eligibility mask. DQN also applied it to learning targets. Added training loss and rolling reward diagnostics.
    Computation
    Reran A at 64 and 128 units and B, C, and D at 128 units. The five configurations produced 360 held-out episodes and ten replay-checked checkpoints.

    On A, wider DQN rose from 45.83 to 47.17 visits and wider PPO fell from 53.50 to 52.83. Observations already used fixed scaling and clipping. DQN median loss rose from the first to the last quarter in all five configurations. The curves gave no clear evidence of convergence.

    PI feedback · 13:50:39 UTC
    ny asked Optuna to search width, batch size, DQN target-update interval, and PPO rollout length. ny also asked for longer training and loss profiles that show the mean and standard deviation.

  4. Round 4

    Expanded Optuna search and longer training

    PI input 13:50:39 UTC

    20 Nov 2026 · first logged Optuna study 13:57:03, last results file 14:11:15 UTC

    PI input · ny
    ny requested configurable network width, batch size, target-update interval, and rollout length, plus longer training and loss profiles with mean and standard deviation.
    Agent work
    Expanded Optuna search, timed CPU and GPU throughput, and plotted a mean and ±1 sample standard deviation across three independent training seeds in 512-step windows.
    Computation
    Eight 4,096-step trials per agent per layout. Each selected model trained for 24,576 steps per seed and completed 77–241 training episodes per run. DQN on D used CUDA; the other training ran on CPU. The held-out comparison includes 288 episodes.

    The tuned agents trailed Random on held-out reward on all four instances. DQN led RL on C and D, PPO on A and B. On D, DQN completed 168.08 visits and PPO 142.67, compared with 187.83 for Random and 272.42 for ACO.

    PI feedback · 14:19:13 UTC
    “Even random outperforms them.” ny asked to diagnose the schedules, explain DQN’s declining training reward and PPO’s flatter profile, and identify the reward incentives.

  5. Round 5

    Diagnose the learned schedules

    PI input 14:19:13 UTC

    Compute timing unavailable for the diagnostic replays.

    PI input · ny
    ny asked how the two learned policies differed from Random and ACO, why training rewards changed, and what the reward incentivized.
    Agent work
    Separated voluntary deferrals from forced ones, examined wait and deadline choices, reconciled the observed reward to four terms, and replayed paired interventions with weights frozen.
    Computation
    Replayed 192 saved episodes across Random, ACO, DQN, and PPO. Matching held-out seeds controlled the comparisons of no voluntary deferral, shorter waits, and changed action sampling.

    DQN deferred on 62% and 49% of eligible decisions on A and B. On D, mean forecast waiting above the shortest feasible choice was 14.91 minutes for DQN, 21.23 for PPO, 10.22 for Random, and 0.17 for ACO. When an alternative was available, overriding decisions that would wait more than 15 minutes improved reward on all paired seeds for both agents on all four layouts.

    PI feedback · 14:40:47 UTC
    ny interpreted the schedules as evidence of a possible exploration problem and a possible reward-design problem, and asked to test guided exploration and monitor all four reward terms.

  6. Round 6

    Guided exploration and reward decomposition

    PI input 14:40:47 UTC

    20 Nov 2026 · family runner logs 14:46:32–14:53:46 UTC

    PI input · ny
    ny asked for exploration strategies using rules and sometimes ACO solutions, and asked to track four reward components and the weighted sum throughout training.
    Agent work
    Mixed short-wait or bounded ACO choices into DQN’s epsilon exploration. For PPO, used the same teachers to warm-start the policy by imitation, then collected ordinary on-policy rollouts. Logged completed and unserved visits, travel, waiting, and weighted reward during training with the original reward weights.
    Computation
    Ran 36 fits across A and D at 12,288 steps each. Used three training seeds and 12 held-out seeds per variant, yielding 432 learned-policy evaluations. Reused 48 matching Random and ACO baseline evaluations. The route pages show training seed 11, selected before evaluation.

    On D, the ACO mix raised DQN held-out reward from 296.78 to 486.93. The short-wait warm start raised PPO from −104.58 to 58.71. On A, both DQN guides lowered held-out reward and both PPO warm starts yielded small gains. All learned variants scored below Random and ACO on both layouts. For the DQN ACO mix on D, weighted training reward fell from 1,895.35 to 534.31 as waiting rose from 693 to 2,447 nurse-minutes across the plotted windows.

    PI feedback · 14:40:47 UTC
    Before the run, ny identified exploration and reward design as possible causes of long waits. After seeing the curves, ny singled out the weak waiting penalty on C and D and asked about multi-step returns. See the next round.

  7. Round 7

    Waiting costs and longer returns

    PI input 15:36:47 UTC · 15:40:58 UTC

    20 Nov 2026 · family runner logs 15:43:11–15:55:28 UTC

    PI input · ny
    ny highlighted similar travel times but large numbers of unserved visits and much longer waits, particularly in C and D. ny asked about multi-step or Monte Carlo rewards and approved the proposed tests.
    Agent work
    Compared stronger waiting penalties and longer return horizons as separate changes. DQN used 16- and 32-decision replay targets with the tuned discount. PPO used λ=1 with its tuned discount and a separate undiscounted λ=1 arm. Every PPO arm used 512-decision rollouts. A rollout ending mid-shift still bootstrapped. All arms treated the modeled shift end as terminal for learning.
    Computation
    Ran 60 fits across C and D at 12,288 steps each, with 3 training seeds and 12 held-out seeds per learned arm. Collected 4,233 complete training episodes and 720 learned-policy evaluations. Reused 48 saved Random and ACO baseline evaluations. The route pages use training seed 11.

    On C, DQN 32-step had the highest reference reward among the learned arms at 534.94. Random scored 187.97 and ACO scored 851.16. On D, DQN 16-step had the highest reference reward among the learned arms at 725.46. Random scored 844.28 and ACO scored 2,456.45.

    PI feedback
    ny then asked whether Round 7 had used the strongest guidance and requested the combined settings for all layouts. See Round 8.

  8. Round 8

    Guided exploration with longer returns

    20 Nov 2026 · family runner logs 16:06:17–16:17:15 UTC

    PI input · ny
    ny requested training both agents on all four instances with longer return estimates, a higher waiting penalty, short-wait guidance, and ACO guidance.
    Agent work
    Kept waiting and return settings fixed within each layout and agent family and compared the exploration control with short-wait and ACO guidance. All arms charged 0.15 points per appointment waiting minute. Masked DQN used 32-decision targets on C and 16-decision targets on A, B, and D, based on Round 7 results for C and D. All PPO arms used 512-decision rollouts with λ=1 and the tuned discount. PPO bootstrapped at rollout boundaries within a shift. The simulator treated the modeled shift end as terminal in training. DQN mixed guidance into epsilon exploration. PPO used imitation warm starts in its guided arms, then collected on-policy rollouts.
    Computation
    Ran 72 fits across A–D, each for 12,288 steps, using three independent training seeds. Recorded 6,309 complete training episodes, evaluated 864 learned-policy shifts, and reused 96 matched Random and ACO shifts. The route explorers use predeclared training seed 11.

    On A, uniform DQN led at the 0.15 score. Both DQN guides reduced waiting and served fewer visits. Short-wait PPO led the learned A arms under the original 0.05 score. On B and C, short-wait DQN was the strongest learned arm on both scales. On D, short-wait PPO was the strongest learned arm, beating Random across all three training seeds. Standalone ACO led on every layout.

    PI diagnostics
    ny asked for a loss and baseline diagnosis, then checked which parts of the scheduling state the agents could see and how far their returns reached. Read the diagnostic findings.
