Algorithm setup

How the dispatch simulator models a home care shift and how Round 10 compares learned policies with fixed scheduling rules.

A simulated home care shift

Nurses leave a common depot to visit patients. Each appointment has a stable ID, a location, a skill requirement, and a service-start window. A patient can have several appointments. The instances use the original workbook data and nurse–patient preference matrices from the associated SHHCSRP study.

Events and time

SimPy runs nurses concurrently. The dispatcher issues one action for the current idle nurse at a time. Several decisions can occur at the same simulation time. The model advances as nurses travel, wait for windows to open, complete care, or return to the depot.

Feasible assignments

An appointment must be unassigned and within the nurse’s skill level. The forecast checks the service-start deadline, a maximum wait of 40 minutes, and enough time to finish and return to the depot within a 480-minute shift.

Uncertain service

Expected service time depends on nurse skill, appointment demand, the nurse–patient preference factor, and previous visits. Actual durations use a normal draw with a standard deviation of 15% of the mean and a minimum of one minute. A feasible forecast can overrun after the service duration is realized.

What a policy sees and does

At each decision, action j assigns the current nurse to appointment j. For an instance with N appointments, action N defers the nurse. The eligibility mask disables invalid assignments and keeps defer available. The dispatcher revisits deferred nurses when the simulator advances to a service completion or an upcoming appointment window.

Numeric observations use fixed scaling to the range [0, 1]. Busy nurse availability is forecast from travel, appointment opening, the duration model, and time already spent in service. A deferred nurse’s estimate depends on the next wake-up event. The interactive simulator exposes the same assignment and defer decisions for a shift you dispatch yourself.

Policies and guidance

Fixed references

Random samples uniformly from feasible appointments. Ant colony optimization (ACO) forecasts short sequences of visits for the current nurse and dispatches the first job from its best sequence. Earlier benchmarks also ran Greedy and Earliest Deadline.

Masked DQN

The agent learns an action value for each appointment ID and defer. Eligibility applies during exploration, greedy selection, and the learning target. The target combines rewards over 64, 128, or 256 decisions before a masked value estimate of the remaining return. Targets stop at the end of the shift.

Maskable PPO

PPO learns a probability distribution over the available actions. Round 10 uses a fixed rollout of 512 decisions and γ = 0.9995. Three λ values vary how strongly future temporal-difference residuals contribute. A rollout ending mid-shift uses a value estimate for the remaining return. The modeled shift end is terminal for learning.

The short-wait teacher ranks feasible appointments by forecast waiting time, closing time, then travel. The ACO teacher uses three ants with a three-visit lookahead. In DQN’s guided exploration, the short-wait arm mixes 75% teacher actions with 25% random legal actions. The ACO arm uses 20% ACO, 55% short-wait, and 25% random legal actions. These proportions apply to exploratory decisions with feasible appointments. PPO warm-starts by imitating short-wait or ACO demonstrations and then collects rollouts from its own policy.

Waiting is time spent after arrival before a visit can start. Deferring a nurse carries no direct penalty, so inspect visits and waiting together. The simulator also penalizes invalid dispatches and explicit missed-window events. The report scores every held-out schedule at both waiting costs so that comparisons use the same weights.

Round 10 used 12,288 decisions per fit with training seeds 11–13 and demonstration seeds 201–204. It used 264 new fits and 24 matching Round 9 DQN fits at 64 decisions with the MLP. Each final checkpoint was evaluated greedily on held-out seeds 101–112, giving 3,456 learned-policy evaluations. The comparison reused 96 matching saved Random and ACO shifts. The benchmark pairs service-time disturbances by appointment and episode seed across policies. Route explorers display training seed 11. Plot bands show sample standard deviation across training seeds.

The benchmark uses Stable-Baselines3 and SB3-Contrib 2.6.0 with SimPy and Gymnasium. Both agents on instance D used a GTX 1660 SUPER. Instances A–C used the CPU.

Scope and provenance

This project is a research simulation. The workbook coordinates and vertical travel rule describe the benchmark layouts. The browser simulator provides hands-on dispatch practice, and the results pages show recorded Python runs. The development history records how the policies, return estimates, and reward weights evolved.

Source study. Zhang et al., Home health care routing and scheduling in densely populated communities considering complex human behaviours. Read the study.
